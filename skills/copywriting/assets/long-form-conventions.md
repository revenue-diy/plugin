# LONG-FORM CONVENTIONS

> **Version 0.1** | 2026-10-15

Articles, guides, case studies, newsletters, and the words of a page or a page section. The page's structure and layout are a design job; this file covers its words. The client's channel and search rules in the context win wherever they differ from this file.

---

## BEFORE THE FIRST WORD

The plan carries the reader, the goal, the angle and the stand it takes, the search term and the questions it answers where the piece must rank, and an outline with a word budget per section.

---

## ARTICLE STRUCTURE

A scaffold, not a formula – drop or merge sections the piece does not need.

| Section | Job |
|---|---|
| **Headline** | promises a specific value to a specific reader; carries the search term where the piece must rank |
| **Opening** | a problem the reader recognises, why it matters now, what they will get from reading on |
| **The problem** | the pain in the reader's own words, and what it costs them |
| **Why the usual fixes fall short** | the stand – where common advice goes wrong, specifically |
| **The approach** | the method, in concrete steps with real examples |
| **Proof** | results, a case or data – each one sourced |
| **What to do next** | the reader's first move, this week |
| **Close** | lands the one idea; one call to action |

---

## PAGE COPY

- **One job per section** – a section that does two is split or cut.
- **Headline states the outcome** for the reader; the subhead adds how, or for whom.
- **Body short**, scannable, in the reader's words.
- **One primary call to action per section**, with proof close to the ask.
- **Each objection answered where it arises**, not in a list at the end.

---

## CRAFT

- **Depth over breadth** – add what only this writer, with this material, can add.
- **Every heading promises a specific value**; a reader scanning the headings alone gets the argument.
- **Scannable and readable** – short paragraphs, clear subheads, bold used sparingly on the claims that carry the piece.
- **Heading hierarchy** – one H1, then H2 and H3 in order.

---

## SEARCH AND AI ANSWERS

Only where the piece must rank or be quoted.

- The search term in the headline, the opening paragraph and one subhead, where it reads naturally – never stuffed.
- Each question the reader searches gets a plain answer in the paragraph under its own heading.
- A title tag and a meta description, within the client's search rules where the context holds them, else the search engine's current guidance.
- Links to the client's related pages, where the material names them.

---

## DELIVERY EXTRAS

Only where they help the piece travel:
- the title tag and meta description (web pieces);
- two short excerpts for social, drawn from the piece's strongest lines.

---

## COMMON FAILURES

- "Studies show" with no named source.
- Vague proof ("many companies see results").
- Hedging where the piece should take a stand.
- Keywords forced into sentences.
- Walls of text, or a close with no next step.
