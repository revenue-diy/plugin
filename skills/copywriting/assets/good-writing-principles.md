# GOOD WRITING PRINCIPLES

> **Version 0.1** | 2026-10-15

The craft every piece follows, whatever its format. The client's brand rules win wherever they differ from this file.

---

## PRINCIPLES

- **One reader, one idea.** Write to one person. The whole point fits one sentence; a second idea is the next piece.
- **Value first.** Open with what the reader gets – a real claim, a number, a named thing. Never tease what you could simply say.
- **Their words.** Use the phrases the reader uses about their own problem, not the category's jargon.
- **Concrete over abstract.** Name the real person, number, tool or moment. An abstraction with no example is cut or given one.
- **Proof travels with the claim.** A specific claim carries its number or named source in the same line. "Many teams" becomes the count and who counted.
- **Every word has a job.** Name the job a word, sentence or section does for the reader. Cut what does none; rewrite a line that does its job weakly.
- **Plain and spoken.** Short words, short sentences, varied length. Read it aloud and rewrite what you stumble on.
- **Active and direct.** A clear subject doing a strong verb. No hedge stacks; one softener at most.
- **Honest.** No hype, no urgency that is not real, no claim you could not stand behind in front of the reader.
- **Earn the next line.** Each line makes the next one worth reading; a line that does not is moved or cut.

---

## EDITORIAL LENSES

Each lens is a reason to rewrite. Apply them to every draft, the user's or yours; where the user's draft is refined, the change log names the lens behind each change.

| Lens | Ask | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Credibility** | Can a stranger tell, from the opening, why this writer can say this? | Swap generic authority ("years of experience") for a specific earned signal from the material – a number, a named client or experiment, a result. None in the material → ask for one. |
| **One main idea** | Can the whole point be said in one sentence? | Cut or park what does not serve it; make the close land that idea. |
| **Topicality** | Is there a reason to read this now? | Anchor to a recent event, number or change the reader knows – only a real one. |
| **Dense opening** | Does the first line deliver a real claim, or tease one? | Move the most concrete point to the first or second line; cut hook-gaps ("here's what nobody talks about"). |
| **Data specificity** | Is every claim backed by a number or a named source? | Add the source from the material, or mark it `[SOURCE NEEDED]`. A hard check: never invent one. |
| **Closer** | Does the last line do work? | End on a question worth answering, a direct ask or a sharp prediction – never a flat summary. |

**Change log** – one line per change that matters, highest leverage first: `**<Lens>**: <what changed, and where>`. Skip lenses that needed no work; never list every tweak.

---

## THE SOPHISTICATED-SLOP TEST

Polished is not the same as good. Ask of every paragraph: would the reader miss it if it were cut? Could only this writer, with this material, have written it? A piece that reads well and says nothing is slop – say so, then rebuild it from the raw material or ask for more.

---

## DEFAULT AI TELLS

The scan list when the brand states no rules of its own.

- Em dashes (U+2014) – a comma, colon or full stop instead.
- "It's not X, it's Y" flips – state the thing directly.
- Throat-clearing openers – "Here's the thing:", "Let me be clear:", "In today's…", "Let's dive in".
- Exactly three of anything, and three-beat slogans ("Build faster. Sell smarter. Scale harder.").
- Hype words – game-changer, unlock, supercharge, skyrocket, elevate, revolutionary, seamless, robust, empower, harness.
- False-discovery moves – "Imagine if…", "Sound familiar?", "But what if there was a better way?".
- Hedge stacks (genuinely, honestly, truly) and rhetorical questions used as filler.
