# COPY FRAMEWORKS

> **Version 0.1** | 2026-10-15

Standard structures for copy that has to persuade. A framework is a scaffold for the draft, never a script the reader can see: pick one, or none, and say which in the plan.

---

## PICK ONE

| The piece | Reach for |
|---|---|
| A headline, subject line or bullet to test | **The 4 Us** |
| A short post, ad or email aimed at a felt pain | **PAS** |
| An email or message selling a change | **Before-After-Bridge** |
| A page or post with one clear action, for a reader not yet interested | **AIDA** |
| A product or service explained line by line | **FAB** |
| A sales page or offer email to a sceptical reader | **The 4 Ps** |
| Any piece that must be remembered – as a review checklist | **SUCCESs** |

None fits → write to the track's shape and the plan's outline; a forced framework reads as one.

---

## AIDA – ATTENTION, INTEREST, DESIRE, ACTION

- **Steps** – Attention: stop the reader with the headline or first line. Interest: keep them with information that is relevant to them. Desire: make them want the outcome, with benefits, proof and story. Action: tell them exactly what to do next.
- **Use it when** – the piece has one clear action and must earn a reader who has not yet decided to care.
- **Skip it when** – the buying journey is long and involves several people; then use it as a lens to review each asset, not as one sequence.
- **Origin** – commonly attributed to E. St. Elmo Lewis, around 1898; the early history is disputed.
- **Sources** – https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIDA_(marketing) · https://copyblogger.com/aida-formula-for-blogging/ · https://spotler.com/glossary/aida-model · https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jmhr/3/2/3_150/_article/-char/ja (the disputed origin)

## PAS – PROBLEM, AGITATE, SOLUTION

- **Steps** – Problem: name a problem the reader already feels. Agitate: make its cost concrete, so leaving it unsolved feels expensive. Solution: present the offer or idea as the way out, with the call to action.
- **Use it when** – the reader has a felt pain and the space is short: a post, an ad, an email, a product description.
- **Skip it when** – the pain is assumed rather than known, or the audience distrusts drama. Agitate with concrete, everyday detail in the reader's own words; overdone, it reads as cringe.
- **Origin** – a long-used direct-response formula with no agreed originator.
- **Sources** – https://copyblogger.com/problem-agitate-solve/ · https://www.crazyegg.com/blog/pas-framework/ · https://copyhackers.com/2015/10/copywriting-formula/

---

## BEFORE-AFTER-BRIDGE

- **Steps** – Before: the reader's situation now, and its pain. After: their world once the problem is solved. Bridge: the offer as the way from one to the other.
- **Use it when** – a short, conversion-led piece, most of all an email, where you know what the reader wants to gain and what they want to stop.
- **Skip it when** – you do not yet know the reader's before and after; the bridge then has nothing to join.
- **Origin** – no agreed originator.
- **Sources** – https://www.campaignmonitor.com/email/bab-copywriting · https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/copywriting-templates

## THE 4 Us – USEFUL, URGENT, UNIQUE, ULTRA-SPECIFIC

- **Steps** – a checklist, not a sequence. Useful: it promises value to this reader. Urgent: it gives a reason to read or act now. Unique: it says something new, or in a way no competitor could. Ultra-specific: it uses precise facts and figures. Score each U from 1 to 4; a line that does not reach 3 or 4 on at least three of them is rewritten.
- **Use it when** – gut-checking a headline, a subject line or a bullet before it goes out.
- **Skip it when** – you want proof a line will win: the score is judgement, and only a live test settles it. Forced urgency breeds doubt.
- **Origin** – Michael Masterson (AWAI's 2001 piece credits him with Bill Bonner), taught by AWAI.
- **Sources** – https://www.awai.com/2001/06/a-review-of-the-4-us/ · https://unbounce.com/copywriting/gut-check-your-landing-page-headlines/ · https://www.bly.com/Pages/DMNCOL1e[1].htm

## FAB – FEATURES, ADVANTAGES, BENEFITS

- **Steps** – Feature: what the product is or does, as a fact. Advantage: what that feature lets the customer do. Benefit: what that means in this customer's own situation – money, time, risk. The fill-in pattern: "Because it has… you can… which means…".
- **Use it when** – product details are listed with no meaning attached: a product page, a one-to-one explanation, an answer to a price objection.
- **Skip it when** – in a large, complex sale, a benefit the buyer has not asked for. Tie each benefit to a need the buyer has said out loud; an unrequested one invites objections.
- **Origin** – a long-standing sales-training convention with no agreed originator; SPIN selling research narrows it: a benefit counts when it meets a need the buyer has stated.
- **Sources** – https://www.sellingpower.com/2010/02/02/8554/understanding-feature-advantage-benefit · https://mailchimp.com/resources/copwriting-formulas · https://www.huthwaiteinternational.com/blog/spin-selling-applies-science-to-the-art-of-selling

## THE 4 Ps – PROMISE, PICTURE, PROOF, PUSH

- **Steps** – Promise: the claim of what the reader gets. Picture: a vivid image of their life with it. Proof: testimonials, data or cases that back the promise. Push: one clear call to action.
- **Use it when** – a sales page or an offer email to a reader who needs convincing.
- **Skip it when** – a home page or a lead-capture page, where a painted picture can read as hype; never promise what the proof cannot carry. A variant opens with the Picture before the Promise.
- **Origin** – two step orders are in use: the Picture-first order is attributed to Henry Hoke Sr. by two of these pages, with no primary text found; the Promise-first order is taught by AWAI with no originator named.
- **Sources** – https://www.awai.com/2012/03/capture-your-prospects-attention/ · https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/copywriting-templates · https://mailchimp.com/resources/copwriting-formulas · the Picture-first variant, with when to use and skip it: https://copyhackers.com/2015/10/copywriting-formula/ · https://copywriting.school/formulas/pppp-picture-promise-prove-push

## SUCCESs – THE MADE TO STICK CHECKLIST

- **Steps** – a checklist, with no running order. Simple: the core of the idea, stripped down. Unexpected: a surprise that wins attention. Concrete: people, numbers, scenes. Credible: a reason to believe it. Emotional: a reason to care. Stories: a narrative that lets people act on the idea.
- **Use it when** – reviewing any post, pitch or case study for whether it will be remembered.
- **Skip it when** – you need a structure to draft in; pair it with a drafting framework from `§ PICK ONE`.
- **Origin** – Chip Heath and Dan Heath, *Made to Stick* (2007).
- **Sources** – https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Made_to_Stick · https://ssir.org/books/reviews/entry/review_made_to_stick
